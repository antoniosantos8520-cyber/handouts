The situation

Read this first

The Picts are not trespassing here. They are working — quietly, efficiently, and with a technique the party does not have.

Six of them are out on the boards harvesting: two at D, two at K, one on watch at E, one running baskets to F. Four more are in and around the huts. They keep their voices down, stay on the planks, and never splash.

Every one of them is streaked head to foot in a foul gray-brown paste made from the dung heap at A. That is the whole encounter in one image, and the players should see it before anyone says a word about it.

What is already on the table

  • Two harvested blossoms in a woven basket at F. Center Platform — the easiest flowers in the settlement, and already someone else's.
  • Four growing flowers — two at D, two at K.
  • One prepared skin-bag of paint inside H. Lone Hut. The single most valuable object here.
  • Bone knives, scrapers and wrapping leaves for handling both substances.

She is already here

Not a spawn · not a punishment

The Veiled Mother, a Gargantuan Jungle Dragon, lies submerged beneath the walkways between F, G and I. She is the thing whose tracks the party followed in.

She killed the dragon carcass on the trail because it crossed into her water. She did not eat it. That was territory, not hunger, and the party should be able to work that out from the corpse.

She is not held in reserve behind a failed roll. She is under the boards from the first second of the scene, and the encounter is built so the party can learn that before it costs them anything.

Three signs, before anyone sees her

  • A broad wake moves beneath the planks, though no wind touches the water.
  • Something heavy bumps the supports under F. The whole platform shifts an inch.
  • A gull lands, pecks once at something floating, and is gone. It never gets as far as a cry.

The Picts barely look up. One of them pauses, presses fresh paint over his forearms and throat, and goes back to work.

That is the warning and the lesson in the same beat. Do not explain it.

What the party can see

No roll required

The party enters at B and is unnoticed until they do something careless. Give them time to watch. None of the following should sit behind a check:

  • The Picts have deliberately coated themselves in something taken from the dung heap.
  • They do not wash, and they do not enter deep water.
  • They are harvesting two things — flowers and dung.
  • Something very large is moving under the pools.

A strong Wits Check earns the one piece that matters: the paste is masking their scent. It is not repelling insects, not warding off poison, not keeping the heat off. It is making them smell like part of her territory.

The danger track

Three steps · never the same check twice

Do not ask for repeated stealth rolls. Escalate on fiction. A shouted word, a heavy footfall, a missed ranged attack, a plank giving way, or anyone setting foot in the water moves the track one step. It never moves back.

IFirst

The Pools Stir

Trigger — any noise, any splash, any miss

The Veiled Mother starts moving beneath the party. Ripples converge on their position from three directions at once, which is the party's first hint of how big she is.

The lookout at E knows something is wrong. He does not yet know it is them.

IISecond

Something Beneath

Trigger — a second disturbance

She strikes without fully surfacing. A snout, a tail, or one enormous claw comes up through the black water. Snap at an isolated character, take out a run of walkway, or seize a Pict whose paint has washed thin — that last one is worth more than any warning you could speak aloud.

The Picts now know there are strangers here. They do not charge. They fall back toward E and I, working to leave the unpainted newcomers between themselves and her.

IIIThird

The Veil Breaks

Trigger — a third disturbance, or any attack on her

She comes up whole, at G. Dirt Pit or straight through F. Center Platform. Now there are three sides in this fight and none of them are allies.

She takes the nearest creature that does not carry her scent. The Picts shoot from the platforms, grab what they can, and actively try to use the party as bait. The party chooses: fight, run, rob the place, or sprint for A and start smearing.

The paint

The ugly thing is the valuable thing

It does not blind her. It makes a coated character smell like part of her territory instead of like food.

Using it

A character who spends an Action thoroughly coating exposed skin and clothing causes the Veiled Mother to hesitate and pick a different target.

A hurried handful across face and chest buys a moment. The prepared bag in H is reliable and covers one character properly.

It stops working when they

  • attack her,
  • go into the water,
  • take a bad wound and start bleeding freely,
  • or use fire against her.

That last clause is the trap worth setting. The Fire Lotus is right there, and burning it is the most obvious idea anyone will have.

How the Picts fight

They have harvested here before

They know they cannot kill her. They will not try unless there is nothing else left. Once she surfaces, their priorities in order are:

  • Keep the prepared paint. Everything else is replaceable.
  • Get out with the flowers.
  • Kill or abandon anyone in the way of the first two.

They fight like people who work here. A cornered Pict throws the flower basket away from himself and runs, expecting the party to scatter after it. Another cuts a support rope instead of swinging. A third shoves a character into the water rather than make a conventional attack — which is both an attack and a summons.

The move that teaches the whole scene: a Pict grabs a character and drags them toward the deep water, not to drown them, but to wash them.

The clean win

What good play looks like

Careful players can walk out of here with all six blossoms, raw dung enough to paint the whole party, the Picts' prepared bag, the knowledge of why it works, and confirmation that far bigger things than the Veiled Mother live on this island.

They never have to fight her. The best possible outcome is theft, comprehension, and a quiet exit — leaving the Picts and the dragon behind them to sort each other out.

Built this way, the scene produces stealth, observation, robbery, environmental tactics, a fight, and a flight, arriving in whatever order the party causes. Nobody picked them off a menu beforehand.

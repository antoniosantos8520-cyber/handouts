Maw of the Mountain

Weaponless captives of the Fire Serpent Tribe must break their bonds, flee a Pictish island, and cheat the burning god of the volcano before the blood moon claims them.

System Blood and Steel Length 1–2 sessions Type Escape / open sandbox Party starts with no gear Foes Picts & jungle dragons
✶ · ✦ · ✶

GM Prep Overview & The Spine

A quick-to-prep, easy-to-run Tale. It works well as a first game or to fold new characters into an existing group: everyone wakes up captured together, and each fell into Pictish hands in their own way. Invite every player to describe how their character was taken. The pitch to the table is brutal and simple: escape captivity, or become a human sacrifice.

The Situation

  • The PCs are captives of the Fire Serpent Tribe, a notoriously vicious band of Picts, on a small nameless tropical island (the Isle of Fire) off the southern coast of the Pictish Wilderness.
  • They begin weaponless and unarmored, wrists bound, held in a hide-flapped hut. Reassure players they can win their gear back.
  • A blood moon rises tonight. At sunset the Picts return to march their prisoners to the volcano for a mass sacrifice. It is currently midmorning.
  • Escape means three things: break their bonds → flee the encampment → find a way off the island.

Run It Like This

  • Open in the hut (Scene 1). After the break-out the island is an open sandbox — let the PCs discover locations as they reach them.
  • The only fixed beats are the start (captivity) and the possible finale at the altar. Everything between is player-driven.
  • Enemy counts are per-PC dials, not fixed numbers. Scale to the party's strength and how bloodied they already are.
  • Picts are lean and aggressive. Reinforcements can always "swarm back from the ritual site" to keep tension high.
Tribe-wide rule · Eyes of the Fire Serpent Every member of the Fire Serpent Tribe has remarkable eyes. All Picts in this Tale gain a +2 Modifier to any Edge Check or Contest involving vision (spotting sneaks, winning stealth Contests, etc.). Factor this into every sneak attempt below.

From the hut, three directions beckon. None is mandatory except the escape itself. The volcano is the "dungeon"; the Shore is the exit; the Pools are the optional treasure detour.

Scene 1 Escape!

The opening beat. The PCs wake bound in a stinking hut with a forced march behind them and a sacrifice ahead. Feet are free; wrists are tied; the door is only a flap of hide. There are guards, though how many depends on party size, time of day, and the path they choose out.

The interior of the hut reeks of sour bodies and damp earth. You manage only shallow breaths and have exchanged few words with the others around you. A brutal forced march through the jungle brought you to this encampment, leaving you sweaty, blistered, and exhausted. Now you must conserve your remaining strength, for when that billowing hut flap opens again, you will be hauled out under the burning sun and led off to your doom.

Will the Picts of the Fire Serpent Tribe take you to some dark-stained altar? Will your neck sweat under the curved blade of a cackling sorcerer? Or will your captors simply toss you into the burning maw of the mountain? You have heard enough tales of the Picts to know what happens in their primordial hell. And what you have gleaned of this Fire Serpent Tribe suggests you were not brought here for a celebration, at least not one where you take part in the merriment. These brutes are infamous even among other Picts. It is said they were born from soil long tainted by the bones of rare fire serpents. You know only that you are to be sacrificed, unless you seize this last chance to break your bonds and make your escape back to the western coast.

Just now, two Picts can be heard outside, conversing in their unusual tongue.

Listening to the guards

Two Picts talk just outside. A PC who listens closely makes a Wits Check. A PC who has spent time among Picts may get +2 (GM's call), but the Fire Serpent tongue is nearly unknown to outsiders. Reveal by threshold:

Wits Diff 5
They are to be sacrificed at the "maw of the mountain" — the smoking volcano they glimpsed on arrival. The camp is mostly quiet; besides the two talkers, few Picts seem to be around.
Wits Diff 7
Talk of the "veiled pools" (a nearby swamp) where flowers bloom with healing and intoxicating properties. A mass sacrifice happens tonight as the blood moon rises.
Wits Diff 10
They speak of the "special one," a woman among the sacrifices. The Picts chatter excitedly at the prospect of her death.
Clock It is midmorning, hours before noon. The Picts will not come back for the prisoners until just before sunset. That is the escape window.

Breaking free & slipping out

Burst the bonds

Might Diff 9 or Edge Diff 9

A Might success breaks the cords; an Edge success lets them wriggle free. Once one PC is loose, they may free the others with no further Check.

Peer through the flap

Edge Diff 5 — sees the shadows of the two talking Picts; no other sign of life.

Edge Diff 10 — spots two clubs leaned against the hut, within reach; hears more voices to the south: there are more than just the two nearby.

Sneaking out · Edge Contest To leave the hut (or send a lone scout to grab weapons), each PC who exits rolls an Edge Contest against the nearby Picts — who enjoy their +2 Eyes of the Fire Serpent bonus. If all PCs succeed, they slip away undetected for now. If the Picts win even one Contest, the party is spotted: roll Initiative. A successful scouting run can recover the party's confiscated gear, or at least an assortment of the Picts' own weapons.

Who is guarding the camp

Most of the tribe is up at the volcano preparing the ritual, so the camp is thin. Use the dial below; if the PCs make light work of the guards, have reinforcements or "waves swarming back from the ritual site" arrive to keep the pressure on.

Book guidance: 1–2 Pict Hunters per PC as a starting point; for stronger parties fold in Pict Warriors or a Pict Champion. Full stat blocks in the Bestiary.

Exits Once loose, the PCs choose a heading: toward the volcano → The Sacred Path; back to the beach → The Shore; into the jungle → The Veiled Pools.

Scene 2 The Shore & The Wreckage

The beach where the PCs first landed. Their captors' boats are gone (returned to sea, leaving no drag-trails in the sand). Among the rotting hulls hides Silas — the party's ticket off the island and their walking briefing document.

The beach is desolate. Other than the footprints marking your previous journey, there are no other signs of trespass. To the south you see a distant cape jutting from the verdant jungle. A short distance away, peeking out from the sea, are what look to be the shattered remains of ships. Scanning the shore in the other direction reveals a busy horizon: seabirds wheel overhead and descend on something dead on the beach.

The Wreckage & meeting Silas

Numerous ships rot on the beach, one more just offshore. Hiding among them is Silas, a Zingaran privateer shipwrecked here almost a year ago. His crew was captured and killed; he has since learned the routines of the Fire Serpent Tribe and stayed out of sight. He is nearly finished repairing a piecemeal escape vessel. He will spy on the PCs first and only reveal himself once he is sure they are not allied with the Picts.

You count three, perhaps four, shattered vessels among the wreckage. Some have rotted on the shore for decades. But one among them seems much newer. Its mast stretches up to a crow's nest, resting against the side of the cape. From over the rim, a bearded face stares back at you.

He is no Pict, but a Zingaran if you had to guess. His eyes go wide and a grin splits his jaw. "Mitra's blessed me; you are not Picts!" He raises his hands to show he is unarmed.

Playing Silas Ecstatic and helpful: fresh arms mean he can finish the boat and pull an oar. He is not a warrior and says so plainly. A trained navigator and shipbuilder, he will do everything to avoid a fight with the Picts, but he trades knowledge freely. See his NPC card.

What Silas knows (the info dump)

⚓ Under Construction

His vessel — essentially an oversized canoe built from scrap — is in its last stages of repair. A few more days' work and it is sea-ready. The surrounding waters are rocky and turbulent, but he is confident it can reach the Baracha Isles or even Zingaran shores.

🌑 Blood Moon & the Mountain

When the sun falls today, a blood moon rises and a great sacrifice takes place at the volcano. The "god" is likely just the active volcano. It is reached only by a narrow path through the seaside cliffs, which the superstitious Picts walk only when painted in volcanic ash and dragon dung.

👑 The Duke's Daughter

Among the captives is the daughter of a Zingaran duke — Silas recognized a family sigil as she was carried ashore. How the Picts took her is a mystery, but her rescue would earn a rich man's gratitude.

🐊 The Veiled Pools

He warns against the swamp. Yes, healing lotus flowers grow there and sell for a high price, but dragons — thought long extinct — prowl the water. There are also dragon droppings, which pair with the island's volcanic ash to make the Picts' protective paint.

Scene 3 · Optional The Veiled Pools

A fully optional detour into a drowned Pictish village. The prize is the Fire Lotus (potent healing) and the dragon dung needed to fake the Picts' sacred body-paint. The danger is the dragons — Hyborian "dragons" being enormous dinosaurs, not fire-breathers. Make the flowers scarce and hard-won.

Approach — the dead beast

Gulls feast on a dead "dragon" roughly twice the size of a bull. Whatever killed it had no interest in eating it. Large footprints lead away and stop at a range of mangroves.

Reading the tracks Wits Diff 6 reveals the tracks come from a large quadruped bigger than the dead one — perhaps the result of a territorial battle between two great beasts. The flooded mangrove path leads to the Veiled Pools, where dragons dwell.

As you plunge deeper into the flooded trail, a creeping fog thickens and obscures your vision. The surrounding jungle presses closer, walling you in, forcing you through narrower natural passageways. The leafy canopy knits itself into a veil above, allowing only scattered shards of light to spark from the glassy pools at your feet.

After a few minutes a clearing opens ahead. The treetops still shield the light and the fog remains thick, but the terrain is clearer. You see the remnants of an old village: rotting plankways lead to dilapidated structures balancing on patches of high ground amid the night-black water. Two features keep snatching your attention — clusters of flame-yellow flowers, and large piles of fresh dung.

Two ways to run it — pick one

Option A · Harvesters

A group of Picts is already here harvesting dung and Fire Lotus. The PCs can steal the gathered bounty, fight for it, or both. At a dramatic moment, send in Lesser Jungle Dragons (about one per two PCs) or a single Gargantuan Jungle Dragon to force a fight-or-flight decision.

Option B · Too Quiet…

No Picts, no dragons — a serene scene, until the PCs make noise. Sneaky harvesting is an Edge Diff 9 Check, and the Difficulty climbs the more they take. The first failed Check summons a Gargantuan Jungle Dragon or a small group of Lesser Jungle Dragons.

Keep it deadly, keep it optional The dragons are genuinely lethal. Remind players that fleeing is always a viable option. Keep the Fire Lotus scarce: no more than 5–10 flowers total, and only if the PCs earn them by risking their hides. Dragon stat blocks are in the Bestiary.

Scene 4 The Sacred Path

The bone-lined trail up the seaside cliffs to the volcano, walked by the Picts only when leading tributes to the maw — and only while wearing their "protective" paint of ash and dragon dung. Somewhere along it, the PCs meet a patrol, and a nasty trap waits in the trees.

Meeting a Pict patrol

At some point the PCs cross a group of Picts. How they react hinges on the paint (info the PCs can glean from Silas):

  • PCs wear no paint: the Picts are alarmed — they may attack in a frenzy, flee, or hang back and shadow the party to see where they go.
  • PCs wear the real paint (disguised as Picts): any actual Picts make a Wits Diff 7 Check to unmask them. On a success, how they play it is up to you — perhaps they bide their time until the PCs are surrounded at the volcano, or perhaps they attack at once.
  • PCs fake the paint (not real ash + dragon dung): the Picts notice as soon as the party comes within Close range.
Sizing the patrol The book fixes no number here on purpose. Read the party's condition: if they are already battered or low on Life/Stamina from earlier fights, keep this patrol small so they can save their strength for the finale. A workable default is a handful of Hunters and Warriors, optionally led by a Champion, scaled to taste.

The trap — the spiked log

A single trip wire triggers a spiked log that drops from the trees and sweeps along the trail.

Edge Diff 8
Lead PC only. Pass: spots the trip wire and avoids the trap. Fail: triggers it and automatically suffers 2D10 Damage.
Edge Diff 6
Up to two PCs standing near the one who tripped it. Pass: they move out of the way. Fail: 1D10 Damage each.

Follow the path far enough and it delivers the party to The Maw of the Mountain.

Scene 5 · Climax The Maw of the Mountain

Beyond a short cave system, the sacrificial site: a carmine-stained altar on a promontory over a pool of magma. The other tributes are already gone. Only Sofia, the duke's daughter, remains in a cage — and a Pict sorcerer is calling on fiends as the blood moon climbs.

The heat · on entry The chamber's heat is oppressive. Each PC who enters makes a Grit Diff 6 Check or suffers −1 to all Checks, Contests, and Attacks until they leave the Area. Call for this the moment they step in.

Through stinging eyes you try to count the darkened figures standing before an undulating curtain of fire. Far below, an inferno simmers, hungry to consume anything that slips over the edge of the promontory. The mountain has already eaten this night, but there is one course yet to come: a girl, cowering in a wooden cage, tears streaking her ash-smeared face.

She does not notice you, nor does the horde of Picts surrounding an altar at the lip of the ledge. Before this stands a sorcerer, arms raised high, tendrils of blood trickling and swaying from his hands. You sense his gestures are more than theatrics; he is calling on fiends to aid his sorcery, and you have only a breath to decide your next move.

The only features are the altar and two wooden cages flanking the cave entrance. By now every other sacrifice has been made; only Sofia is left.

Rescuing Sofia by stealth

A capable scout might slip over and free the girl before the Picts notice. Any PC attempting the rescue rolls an Edge Contest against the nearby Picts — remember their +2 Eyes of the Fire Serpent bonus. Every PC who joins the attempt must roll.

  • All PCs succeed: they free Sofia and gain a head start before the Picts react — or they seize the moment for a Surprise Attack.
  • Picts win even one Contest: the party is detected. Everyone rolls Initiative.
Sofia in the fight The Picts will not attack Sofia — she "belongs to the Maw." They may, however, try to restrain her and drag her back to the altar or cage. She is a terrified non-combatant, not a fighter.

The battle — field this

PartyRecommended finale forceIf the PCs are advanced / formidable
3 PCs3 Hunters + 3 Warriors + 1 Sorcerer+1 Pict Champion
4 PCs4 Hunters + 4 Warriors + 1 Sorcerer+1–2 Pict Champions
5 PCs5 Hunters + 5 Warriors + 1 Sorcerer+2–3 Pict Champions

Book guidance: at least one Pict Hunter and one Pict Warrior per PC, plus the Pict Sorcerer; add 1–3 Pict Champions for advanced parties. Add foes wherever you need them to hold the tension.

Endless tide Do not fix a number of Picts on the island. More may lurk nearby or at distant sites. The players should feel that even if they slay everyone at the altar, more could be waiting to take their place. Fleeing: if the PCs run, you may call a Grit Contest to outrun their foes; whoever scores lowest is caught by a few Attacks on the way out.

Finale Getaway from the Isle of Fire

Rescued Sofia or not, the PCs still have to leave. Silas and his patched boat are their best hope. Let the players choose their own road to survival and describe the closing action.

Run for the boat

If the PCs return to Silas with Picts in pursuit, you may rule the vessel is sea-ready — especially if a PC stayed behind to help with repairs. A dramatic finish: Pictish arrows hissing into the sea just short of the fleeing canoe.

Stand and fight

The PCs may pick a battleground to their advantage, hold the line, and cut down every Pict still chasing them before they board.

Steal a boat

Ambush a group of Picts returning to the island by their own boats, slay the crew, and take their vessel.

Payoff Conclusion & Rewards

Experience

  • Each surviving PC: 1 XP, plus up to 1 more XP for great roleplaying or clever problem-solving.
  • If they rescue Sofia: +1 XP each, and they secure the favor of a prominent Zingaran duke.

The Duke's Gratitude

See Sofia safely to the mainland and the duke offers sanctuary in his lands plus silver and gold — enough to buy any armor or weapons they like and live extravagantly for months. For a campaign turning toward piracy, he might even provide the means to acquire their own ship.

Fire Lotus · lasting reward Any harvested Fire Lotus is noted on the character sheets. Whenever a PC uses a dose during a Recovery, in addition to the normal benefits they gain +1 Stamina Point and regain an additional 2D6 Life Points.

Cast Key NPCs

⚓
Ally · Non-combatant

Silas — Zingaran Privateer

Shipwrecked on the Isle of Fire almost a year ago; his crew was captured and killed. A trained navigator and shipbuilder (his privateering was, embarrassingly, mostly navigation). He has survived by learning the Fire Serpent Tribe's routines and staying unseen, and is close to finishing a scrap-built escape boat. Wants: off this island — the PCs are extra hands to finish and row the vessel. Will not fight the Picts, but trades everything he knows freely. He is the party's exit and their briefing.

👑
Objective · Non-combatant

Sofia — The Duke's Daughter

Daughter of a prominent Zingaran duke, identified by Silas from a family sigil as she was carried ashore. She is the last sacrifice remaining, caged at the altar, ash-smeared and terrified. The Picts regard her as belonging to the Maw: they will not kill her out of turn, but will try to drag her back to the altar or cage if the PCs intervene. Rescuing and returning her earns the duke's lasting favor — and his fortune.
